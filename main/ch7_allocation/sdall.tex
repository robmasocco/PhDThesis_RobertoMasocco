To investigate this, consider the continuous-time, linear time-invariant plant $\plant$ given by
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:sdall_ctsys}
  \begin{align}
    \dot{\plantstate}(t) &= \plantdynmat \plantstate(t) + \plantctrmat \plantinput(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\\
    \plantoutput(t) &= \plantoutmat \plantstate(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\plantstate(t)\in\R^{\plantstatedim}$ is the plant state vector, $\plantinput(t)\in\R^{\plantinputdim}$ is the plant input vector, and $\plantoutput(t)\in\R^{\plantoutputdim}$ is the plant output vector at time $t$, and $\plantdynmat\in\R^{\plantstatedim\times\plantstatedim}$, $\plantctrmat\in\R^{\plantstatedim\times\plantinputdim}$, and $\plantoutmat\in\R^{\plantoutputdim\times\plantstatedim}$ are constant matrices. Let $\tau_s\in\R{\geq 0}$ be the sampling time. The plant \eqref{eq:sdall_ctsys} is part of a feedback control scheme comprising a sampled-data controller $\ctrl$ described by the following discrete-time, linear time-invariant state-space model:
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:sdall_dtcontroller}
  \begin{align}
      \ctrlstate_{k+1} &= \ctrldynmat\ctrlstate_k + \ctrlctrmat\plantoutput_k + \ctrlrefmat\bbref_k, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0},\\
      \ctrlouput_k &= \ctrloutmat\ctrlstate_k + \ctrldirgain\plantoutput_k + \ctrlrefgain\bbref_k, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0},
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\ctrlstate_k\in\R^{\ctrlstatedim}$ is the controller state vector, and $\ctrlouput_k\in\R^{\ctrloutputdim}$ is the controller output vector at time $k$. The matrices $\ctrldynmat\in\R^{\ctrlstatedim\times\ctrlstatedim}$, $\ctrlctrmat\in\R^{\ctrlstatedim\times\plantoutputdim}$, $\ctrlrefmat\in\R^{\ctrlstatedim\times\bbrefdim}$, $\ctrloutmat\in\R^{\ctrloutputdim\times\ctrlstatedim}$, $\ctrldirgain\in\R^{\ctrloutputdim\times\plantoutputdim}$, and $\ctrlrefgain\in\R^{\ctrloutputdim\times\bbrefdim}$ are constant. $r(t)\in\R^{\bbrefdim}$ is the reference input at time $t$, generated by an exosystem of the form \eqref{eq:bbexosys}. Let $\dplantdynmat\in\R^{\plantstatedim\times\plantstatedim}$, $\dplantctrmat\in\R^{\plantstatedim\times\plantinputdim}$, and $\plantoutmat\in\R^{\plantoutputdim\times\plantstatedim}$ be constant matrices given by
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:sdall_discretization}
  \begin{align}
    \dplantdynmat &= e^{\plantdynmat\tau_s},\\
    \dplantctrmat &= \int_{0}^{\tau_s} e^{\plantdynmat(\tau_s - \sigma)}\plantctrmat\,d\sigma,\\
    \dplantoutmat &= \plantoutmat.
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}
The matrices in \eqref{eq:sdall_discretization} yield the following \emph{discretized} state-space description of the plant $\plant$, equivalent to \eqref{eq:sdall_ctsys} at the sampling instants $t=k\tau_s$, $k\in\Z_{\geq 0}$:
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:sdall_dtsys}
  \begin{align}
    \plantstate_{k+1} &= \dplantdynmat\plantstate_k + \dplantctrmat\plantinput_k, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0},\\
    \plantoutput_k &= \dplantoutmat\plantstate_k, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0}.
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}
Let $\{0,\omega_1,\dotsc,\omega_h\}$ be natural pulses such that there exist $\bbperiod\in\R_{>0}$ and $\{k_i\}_{i=1}^{h}\subset\N$ such that
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:sdall_exofreqs}
  \omega_i &= k_i\frac{2\pi}{\bbperiod}, & i=1,\dotsc,h.
\end{align}
The characteristics of the control system resulting from the feedback interconnection of \eqref{eq:sdall_ctsys} and \eqref{eq:sdall_dtcontroller}, as well as the properties of the exosystem considered in this scenario, are stated by the following assumptions.
\begin{assumption}
  \label{ass:sdall_controller}
  The pair $(\plantdynmat,\plantctrmat)$ is controllable, and the pair $(\plantoutmat,\plantdynmat)$ is detectable.
  The design of the discrete-time controller \eqref{eq:sdall_dtcontroller} is based on the discrete-time equivalent description \eqref{eq:sdall_dtsys} of the plant. Moreover, the feedback interconnection of \eqref{eq:sdall_ctsys} and \eqref{eq:sdall_dtcontroller} given by $\plantinput=\ctrlouput$ by means of a \emph{zero-order hold} device on the plant input, and a \emph{sample-and-hold} device on the plant output, each with sampling time $\tau_s$, is well-posed and asymptotically stable.\closestatement
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}
  \label{ass:sdall_exo}
  The reference input $\bbref$ is generated by the exosystem $\bbexo$ given by
  \begin{subequations}
    \label{eq:sdall_ctexo}
    \begin{align}
      \dot{\bbexostate}(t) &= \exodynmat\bbexostate(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\\
      \bbref(t) &= \exooutmat\bbexostate(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},
    \end{align}
  \end{subequations}
  where $\exodynmat\in\R^{\bbexostatedim\times\bbexostatedim}$ is such that
  \begin{subequations}
    \label{eq:sdall_exoblocks}
    \begin{align}
      \exodynmat_0 &= 0,\\
      \exodynmat_i &= \begin{bmatrix}0 & \omega_i \\ -\omega_i & 0\end{bmatrix}, & i=1,\dotsc,h,\\
      \exodynmat &= \diag(\exodynmat_0,\exodynmat_1,\exodynmat_2,\dotsc,\exodynmat_h).
    \end{align}
  \end{subequations}
  The matrix $\exooutmat\in\R^{\bbrefdim\times\bbexostatedim}$ is constant and the pair $(\exooutmat,\exodynmat)$ is observable. Moreover, sampled measurements of the reference input, namely $r_k$ at time $k$, are available by means of a \emph{sample-and-hold} device with the same sampling time $\tau_s$ as in \Cref{ass:sdall_controller}.\closestatement
\end{assumption}
It is worth pointing out that \Cref{ass:sdall_exo} implies that the reference input $r$ is periodic of period $\bbperiod$.
\begin{assumption}
  \label{ass:sdall_periods}
  There exists $K\in\N$ such that $\bbperiod = K\tau_s$.\closestatement
\end{assumption}
Although \Cref{ass:sdall_periods} is instrumental to simplify the following analysis, it can be relaxed in practice as shown later on.

\begin{remark}
  Similarly to the continuous-time, linear case presented in \Cref{sec:all_design_ssinv}, the feedback control system given by \eqref{eq:sdall_ctsys}--\eqref{eq:sdall_dtsys} and \eqref{eq:sdall_ctexo}--\eqref{eq:sdall_exoblocks} can be modeled as in \eqref{eq:bbsys}:
  \begin{subequations}
    \label{eq:sdall_bb}
    \begin{align}
      \plantinput(t) &= \ctrlouput(k\tau_s) + \bbaux(k\tau_s), & t\in [k\tau_s,(k+1)\tau_s), k\in\Z_{\geq 0},\\
      \bbstate(t) &= \begin{bmatrix}\plantstate(t)' & \ctrlstate(k\tau_s)'\end{bmatrix}', & t\in [k\tau_s,(k+1)\tau_s), k\in\Z_{\geq 0},\\
      \bboutp(t) &= \plantinput(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\\
      \bbouto(t) &= \plantoutput(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},
    \end{align}
  \end{subequations}
  where $\bbstate(t)\in\R^{\plantstatedim + \ctrlstatedim}$.\closeremark
\end{remark}

Let $R\in\R^{\plantinputdim\times\plantinputdim}$ be a constant matrix such that $R=R'$, $R\succ 0$. The steady-state plant input is denoted by $\plantssinput$. Consider the following cost functional:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:sdall_ctcost}
  J = \int_{0}^{\bbperiod} \left\Vert\plantssinput(t)\right\Vert^2_{R}\,dt.
\end{equation}
The dynamic input allocation problem for the feedback control system hereby defined is formulated as follows.
\begin{problem}
  \label{prb:sdall_all}
  Consider the sampled-data feedback control system given by \eqref{eq:sdall_ctsys}--\eqref{eq:sdall_dtsys} interconnected as in \eqref{eq:sdall_bb}, and \eqref{eq:sdall_ctexo}--\eqref{eq:sdall_exoblocks}. Let \Cref{ass:bboveract,ass:sdall_periods,ass:sdall_exo,ass:sdall_controller} hold. Find, if any, a \emph{discrete-time input allocation device} (namely, an \emph{allocator}) such that, when interconnected to the feedback loop according to
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_all_interconn}
    \allin &= \bbref, & \allout &= \bbaux,
  \end{align}
  yielding the plant input $\plantinput = \ctrlouput + \allout$, ensures that the following requirements hold.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item[\emph{(I)}] \emph{Sampled output invisibility:} the regulated outputs both in presence and absence of the allocator are such that
      \begin{align}
        \label{eq:sdall_sdinv}
        \bbouto(k\tau_s) &= \bboutoa(k\tau_s), & k\in\Z_{\geq 0}.
      \end{align}
    \item[\emph{(O)}] \emph{Steady-state optimality:} the cost functional \eqref{eq:sdall_ctcost} is brought to its minimum value.
  \end{itemize}
  \closestatement
\end{problem}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:sdall_fullinvprb}
  The statement of \Cref{prb:sdall_all} is indeed a sampled-data variant of \Cref{prb:fullinv}. Although the two problems share the steady-state optimality requirement, what differentiates them is the full output invisibility one. From a continuous-time perspective, \emph{i.e.}, the time domain of the plant \eqref{eq:sdall_ctsys}, \Cref{prb:fullinv} requires the effect of the allocator to be invisible in the plant output at all times. Conversely, \Cref{prb:sdall_all} requires the effect of the allocator to be invisible only at the sampling instants. Conceptually, this difference is motivated by the fact that the control system is implemented using digital devices that are now modeled with discrete-time dynamics; this fact reasonably leads to the formulation of the full output invisibility requirement given in \Cref{prb:sdall_all}.\closeremark
\end{remark}

This scenario, completely new in the literature to the best of the author's knowledge, poses two questions which are the subject of the following investigation: how the structure of the allocator introduced in \Cref{sec:all_design_fullinv} (see \Cref{fig:fullallocator}) has to be adapted to a sampled-data design, and how the allocator action affects the plant output between the sampling instants \cite{galeani_dynamic_2022}.

\begin{example}
  \label{ex:sdall_ctann}
  The necessity of a discrete-time allocator design approach to solve \Cref{prb:sdall_all} is highlighted by the following numerical example. Consider the continuous-time, linear time-invariant plant of the form \eqref{eq:sdall_ctsys} given by
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_cex_ctsys}
    \plantdynmat &= \begin{bmatrix}
      -8 & 2 & -9\\
      9 & -12 & 8\\
      5 & -7 & -3
    \end{bmatrix}, & \plantctrmat &= \begin{bmatrix}
      4 & 7\\
      -10 & -6\\
      10 & -7
    \end{bmatrix}, & \plantoutmat &= \begin{bmatrix}
      -7 & -4 & 1
    \end{bmatrix}.
  \end{align}
  An inspection of \eqref{eq:sdall_cex_ctsys} reveals that the plant is asymptotically stable since $\spec(\plantdynmat)\subset\C_{< 0}$, that the pair $(\plantdynmat,\plantctrmat)$ is controllable, and that the pair $(\plantoutmat,\plantdynmat)$ is observable. Let
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_cex_sine}
    v(t) &= 5\sin(10t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},
  \end{align}
  where $v(t)\in\R$ since \eqref{eq:sdall_cex_ctsys} has degree of redundancy $\bbreddeg = 1$. Then, design a \emph{polynomial annihilator} for \eqref{eq:sdall_cex_ctsys} following the procedure described in \Cref{prop:ddall_annih_computation}. Fix the sampling time $\tau_s = \SI{0.1}{\second}$. Let $u_a$ be the input and $\allout$ be the output of the resulting annihilator; interconnect the plant \eqref{eq:sdall_cex_ctsys} and the annihilator such that
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_cex_interconn}
    u_a(t) &= v(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\\
    \plantinput(t) &= \allout(k\tau_s), & t\in [k\tau_s,(k+1)\tau_s), k\in\Z_{\geq 0},
  \end{align}
  \emph{i.e.}, the annihilator output is fed to the plant \eqref{eq:sdall_cex_ctsys} by means of a zero-order hold device with sampling time $\tau_s$. The situation hereby described is depicted in \Cref{fig:sdall_dtann}. The output of \eqref{eq:sdall_cex_ctsys} in presence of its annihilator subject to the sinusoidal input \eqref{eq:sdall_cex_sine} is shown in \Cref{fig:sdall_y_ctzct}.

\Cref{ex:sdall_ctann} suggests that the entire annihilator design approach should be revised in a discrete-time setting when system \eqref{eq:bbsys} involves sampled-data devices. Let $z$ be a variable taking values in $\C$, and $\dplanttf(z)\in\C^{\plantoutputdim\times\plantinputdim}$ be the transfer function of the discretized plant \eqref{eq:sdall_dtsys}. The approach outlined in \cite{cristofaro_output_2014} and further characterized in \Cref{prop:ddall_annih_computation} is extended to the discrete-time case as follows.

\begin{proposition}
  \label{prop:sdall_annih_computation}
  Let $N_a(z)\in\C^{\ctrloutputdim\times\bbreddeg}$ be a polynomial matrix whose columns form a minimal polynomial basis for the null space of $\dplanttf(z)$, and $d_a(z)$ be a monic polynomial with degree larger than that of each element of $N_a(z)$ and whose roots all lie in $\C_{<1}$.
  Define
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:sdall_Wa}
    W_a(z) = N_a(z)d_a^{-1}(z).
  \end{equation}
  Then, it holds that
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_annih}
    \dplanttf(z)W_a(z) &= 0, & z\in\C\setminus(\poleset(\dplanttf(z))\cup\poleset(W_a(z))).
  \end{align}
  Furthermore, given any minimal realization $(A_a,B_a,C_a,0)$ of $\,W_a(z)$, the system
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_annss}
    \vartheta_{k+1} &= A_a \vartheta_k + B_a v_k, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0},\\
    \allout_k &= C_a \vartheta_k, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0},
  \end{align}
  where $\vartheta_k\in\R^{n_a}$ is the state vector of the annihilator at time $k$, and $v_k\in\R^{m_a}$, is a \emph{discrete-time annihilator} for plant \eqref{eq:sdall_dtsys}.\closestatement
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:sdall_lcf}
  Suppose that $\dplanttf(z)$ admits a left coprime polynomial factorization \cite[§7.8]{chen_linear_2009} such that
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:sdall_lcf}
    \dplanttf(z) = \dplantlcfd(z)^{-1}\dplantlcfn(z),
  \end{equation}
  where $\dplantlcfd(z)$, $\dplantlcfn(z)$ are polynomial matrices.
  Then, the polynomial matrix $N_a(z)$ in \Cref{prop:sdall_annih_computation} may be selected among the solutions of
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:sdall_lcf_annih}
    \dplantlcfn(z)N_a(z) = 0.
  \end{equation}
  \closeremark
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:sdall_sdinv}
  It is worth pointing out that \eqref{eq:sdall_annss} is a discrete-time system designed such that \eqref{eq:sdall_annih} holds. Hence, the discrete-time annihilator given by \eqref{eq:sdall_annss} satisfies the sampled output invisibility requirement in \Cref{prb:sdall_all}. However, the annihilator \eqref{eq:sdall_annss} does not guarantee output invisibility between sampling instants and the effect of the allocator action on the plant output is not characterized.\closeremark
\end{remark}

The proof of \Cref{prop:sdall_annih_computation} follows a reasoning similar to that of \Cref{prop:ddall_annih_computation}, hence it is omitted. To describe the proposed steady-state optimizer, some preliminary definitions are in order. Recall \Cref{ass:sdall_periods} and consider the following cost functional, obtained as a sampled-data variant of \eqref{eq:sdall_ctcost}:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:sdall_dtsscost}
  J_d = \tau_s\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \plantssinput_k'R\plantssinput_k.
\end{equation}
$\dexodynmat$ is the sampled exogenous dynamics given by
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:sdall_dexodynmat}
  \dexodynmat = e^{\tau_s\exodynmat}.
\end{equation}
Let $n_{IM}=\bbreddeg\bbexostatedim$ and
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:sdall_Aim}
  A_{IM} = I_{\bbreddeg}\otimes\dexodynmat,
\end{equation}
with $A_{IM}\in\R^{n_{IM}\times n_{IM}}$.
Furthermore, let $(\feedbackdynmat,\feedbackctrmat,\feedbackoutmat,0)$ be a minimal realization of the feedback loop having $\ctrlouput$ as output and note that $\feedbackctrmat = \begin{bmatrix}B_\bbref & B_\bbaux\end{bmatrix}$ since the feedback loop has two exogenous inputs, namely $\bbref$ and $\bbaux$.
Select $C_{IM}\in\R^{\bbreddeg\times n_{IM}}$ such that the pair $(C_{IM},A_{IM})$ is observable and solve the following Sylvester equations for $\Pi_{\bar{M}}\in\R^{(\plantstatedim + \ctrlstatedim)\times\bbexostatedim}$, $\Pi_{\bar{N}}\in\R^{n_a \times n_{IM}}$:
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:sdall_inv}
  \begin{align}
    \Pi_{\bar{M}}\dexodynmat &= \feedbackdynmat\Pi_{\bar{M}} + B_{\bbref}\exooutmat,\label{eq:sdall_invmbar}\\
    \Pi_{\bar{N}}A_{IM} &= A_{a}\Pi_{\bar{N}} + B_{\bbaux}C_{IM},\label{eq:sdall_invnbar}
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}
yielding (see, again, \Cref{app:moments} and references therein)
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:sdall_mandn}
  \begin{align}
    \bar{M} &= \feedbackoutmat\Pi_{\bar{M}},\label{eq:mbar}\\
    \bar{N} &= C_{a}\Pi_{\bar{N}},\label{eq:nbar}
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}
with $\bar{M}\in\R^{\bbauxdim\times\bbexostatedim}$, $\bar{N}\in\R^{\bbauxdim\times n_{IM}}$. Let
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:sdall_Z}
  Z = \dexodynmat^{\bbexostatedim}O(\exooutmat,\dexodynmat)^\dagger.
\end{equation}
Finally, define in addition
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:sdall_bigl_zetass_ssoptgains}
  \begin{align}
    \bar{R} &= I_K\otimes R, & \bar{R}\in\R^{\bbauxdim K \times \bbauxdim K},\\
    L &= \begin{bmatrix} L_1 & L_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
      \bar{M} \, \dexodynmat \, Z & \bar{N} \\
      \bar{M} \, \dexodynmat^{2} \, Z & \bar{N} \, A_{IM} \\
      \vdots & \vdots \\
      \bar{M} \, \dexodynmat^{K} \, Z & \bar{N} \,  A^{K-1}_{IM}
      \end{bmatrix}, & L\in\R^{\bbauxdim K \times (\bbexostatedim\bbrefdim + n_{IM})},\label{eq:sdall_bigl}\\
    F_{\zeta} &= -(L_2'\bar{R}L_2)^{-1}L_2'\bar{R}L_1, & F_{\zeta}\in\R^{n_{IM}\times\bbexostatedim\bbrefdim},\label{eq:sdall_fxi}\\
    F_{IM} &= -(L_2'\bar{R}L_2)^{-1}L_2'\bar{R}L_2A_{IM}, & F_{IM}\in\R^{n_{IM}\times n_{IM}},\label{eq:sdall_fim}\\
    A_{\zeta} &= \begin{bmatrix}
      0 & I_{\bbrefdim} & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\
      0 & 0 & I_{\bbrefdim} & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\
      &&& \hspace{-22pt}\cdots \\
      0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & I_{\bbrefdim} \\
      0 &&& \hspace{-22pt}\cdots && 0
      \end{bmatrix}, & A_{\zeta}\in\R^{\bbexostatedim\bbrefdim\times\bbexostatedim\bbrefdim},\label{eq:sdall_azeta}\\
    B_{\zeta} &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \\ I_{\bbrefdim}\end{bmatrix}, & B_{\zeta}\in\R^{\bbexostatedim\bbrefdim\times\bbrefdim}.\label{eq:sdall_bzeta}
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}

The proposed steady-state optimizer is given by
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:sdall_ssopt}
  \begin{align}
    \psi_{k+1} &= (A_{IM} + F_{IM})\psi_k + F_{\zeta}\zeta_k, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0},\label{eq:sdall_psi}\\
    \zeta_{k+1} &= A_{\zeta}\zeta_k + B_{\zeta}\bbref_k, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0},\\
    v_k &= C_{IM}\psi_k, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0},
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\psi_k\in\R^{n_{IM}}$ is the state vector of the steady-state optimizer at time $k$, and $\zeta_k\in\R^{\bbexostatedim\bbrefdim}$ is a buffer of samples of the reference input $\bbref$. The properties of the discrete-time allocator formed by the interconnection of \eqref{eq:sdall_annss} and \eqref{eq:sdall_ssopt} are summarized in the following result.

\begin{theorem}
  \label{thm:sdall_main}
  Consider the sampled-data feedback control system given by \eqref{eq:sdall_ctsys}--\eqref{eq:sdall_dtsys} interconnected as in \eqref{eq:sdall_bb}, and \eqref{eq:sdall_ctexo}--\eqref{eq:sdall_exoblocks}. Let \Cref{ass:bboveract,ass:sdall_periods,ass:sdall_exo,ass:sdall_controller} hold. Then, the allocator given by \eqref{eq:sdall_annss}, \eqref{eq:sdall_ssopt} and interconnected to the feedback loop such that
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_thm_interconn}
    \allin &= \bbref, & \allout &= \bbaux,
  \end{align}
  solves \Cref{prb:sdall_all}.\closestatement
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  The discrete-time annihilator \eqref{eq:sdall_annss} ensures that the sampled output invisibility requirement holds by construction, as stated in \Cref{prop:sdall_annih_computation}. The motivation at the heart of the structure of \eqref{eq:sdall_ssopt} is that, at each time $k$, $\psi$ must be steered towards an optimal trajectory $\psi^\star$ along which the cost functional \eqref{eq:sdall_ctcost} is minimized; the formulation of \Cref{prb:sdall_all} guarantees that such a trajectory exists. Denote the steady-state controller output by $\ctrlssoutput$ and let
  \begin{subequations}
    \label{eq:sdall_pis}
    \begin{align}
      \pi_{k,i} &= \ctrlssoutput_{k+i} + \bar{N}\psi_{k+i}, & i=1,\dotsc,K,\,k\in\Z_{\geq 0},\\
      \pi_k &= \begin{bmatrix} \pi_{k,1} \\ \pi_{k,2} \\ \vdots \\ \pi_{k,K}\end{bmatrix}, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0}.
    \end{align}
  \end{subequations}
  Equations \eqref{eq:sdall_dtsscost}, \eqref{eq:sdall_pis} yield
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_kroncost}
    J_d &= \tau_s\pi_k'\bar{R}\pi_k, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0}.
  \end{align}
  Recall \eqref{eq:sdall_mandn} and note that, by embedding a copy of the modes of the exosystem (namely, embedding a \emph{steady-state generator}), the steady-state optimizer can compute $\pi_k$ at each time $k$ by forward-propagating the current value of the state vector $\psi$ over an interval of $K$ steps according to
  \begin{subequations}
    \label{eq:sdall_ssoptpred}
    \begin{align}
      \psi_{h+1} &= A_{IM}\psi_h + \delta, & h = k,\,\delta\in\R^{n_{IM}},\\
      \psi_{h+1} &= A_{IM}\psi_h, & h = k+1,\dotsc,k+K-1,
    \end{align}
  \end{subequations}
  where it has been assumed that the correction term $\delta$ at time $h$ has been designed to steer the state $\psi$ towards its optimal steady-state trajectory $\psi^\star$. To compute samples of $\ctrlssoutput$ in \eqref{eq:sdall_pis}, a measurement of the state of the exosystem $\bbexostate_k$ is required; since the optimizer operates in feedforward from the reference input $r$ and \Cref{ass:sdall_exo} holds, a buffer $\zeta$ of the form
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_zeta}
    \zeta_k &= \begin{bmatrix}\bbref_{k-\bbexostatedim}' & \bbref_{k-\bbexostatedim+1}' & \cdots & \bbref_{k-1}'\end{bmatrix}'
  \end{align}
  is enough to compute
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_xirec}
    \bbexostate_k &= Z\zeta_k = ZO(\exooutmat,\dexodynmat)\bbexostate_{k-\bbexostatedim}, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0}.
  \end{align}
  This justifies the structure of $L1$ in \eqref{eq:sdall_bigl}, yielding
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_piest}
    \pi_k &= L\begin{bmatrix}\zeta_k' & \psi_k'A_{IM}'+\delta'\end{bmatrix}', & k\in\Z_{\geq 0}.
  \end{align}
  Given $\pi_k$ in \eqref{eq:sdall_piest}, the value of the correction term $\delta$ that brings the cost functional \eqref{eq:sdall_ctcost} to its minimum value is determined by imposing that the gradient of the cost functional \eqref{eq:sdall_dtsscost} with respect to $\delta$ is equal to zero. Recalling \eqref{eq:sdall_kroncost}, this implies
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_nablajdelta}
    2\tau_s(L_2'\bar{R}L_2\delta + L_2'\bar{R}L_1\zeta_k + L_2'\bar{R}L_2A_{IM}\psi_k) &= 0, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0},
  \end{align}
  yielding what is found in \eqref{eq:sdall_ssopt}, that is
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_delta}
    \delta &= -(L_2'\bar{R}L_2)^{-1}(L_2'\bar{R}L_1\zeta_k + L_2'\bar{R}L_2A_{IM}\psi_k), & k\in\Z_{\geq 0}.
  \end{align}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:sdall_comm_delta}
  As expressed by \eqref{eq:sdall_ssopt}, $\delta$ is computed and applied at every time $k$ instead of every $K$ steps as assumed in \eqref{eq:sdall_ssoptpred}. However, this does not invalidate the correctness of the procedure because a single update is necessary to drive $\psi$ to the optimal trajectory $\psi^\star$, namely
  \begin{align}
    F_\zeta\zeta_k + F_{IM}\psi_k = 0, & k > \bbexostatedim + 1,
  \end{align}
  and \eqref{eq:sdall_psi} generates the free response of the discretized exosystem. Moreover, if the closed-loop system goes to steady-state in $N_c$ time steps, and the allocator also does in $N_{all}$ time steps, then the allocated control system goes to steady-state in $\max\{N_c,N_{all}\}$ time steps.\closeremark
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}
  \label{prop:sdall_xss}
  Let
  \begin{subequations}
    \label{eq:sdall_xss}
    \begin{align}
      \plantssinput_k &= \ctrlssoutput_k + \bar{N}\psi_k, & k\in\Z_{\geq 0},\\
      \psi_k^\star &= \arg\min_{\psi}J_d.
    \end{align}
  \end{subequations}
  If the pair $(\bar{N},A_{IM})$ is observable, then the proposed allocator is such that
  \begin{align}
    \psi_k &= \psi^\star_{\mod(k,K)}, & k\geq\bbexostatedim+1.
  \end{align}
  \closestatement
\end{proposition}

Recall that discrete-time systems are termed \emph{deadbeat}, namely such that their transient response goes to zero in a finite number of steps, if and only if all their poles are in zero.

\begin{corollary}
  \label{cor:sdall_ftconv}
  Suppose that the closed-loop given by \eqref{eq:sdall_ctsys}--\eqref{eq:sdall_dtsys} interconnected as in \eqref{eq:sdall_bb} and the annihilator \eqref{eq:sdall_annss} are deadbeat, then the allocated plant input converges to its optimal trajectory in a finite number of steps.\closestatement
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:sdall_unixss}
  $\psi^\star$ is uniquely defined since, in this setting, the cost functional \eqref{eq:sdall_dtsscost} is strictly convex with respect to $\plantssinput$.\closeremark
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:sdall_finitespace}
  Some observations about the space of admissible control laws are in order here. In \cite{galeani_dynamic_2014}, it is observed that these belong to a prescribed set (namely, the modes of the exosystem), which however remains an infinite-dimensional space. Instead, the admissible allocator actions in the present context, hence the optimal control input with respect to \eqref{eq:sdall_ctcost}, lie in a finite-dimensional space because in this setting the controls are sampled, \emph{i.e.}, piecewise-constant signals.\closeremark
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:sdall_tnotinz}
  As anticipated above, \Cref{ass:sdall_periods} may be relaxed in practice provided a few observations are discussed. To begin with, the intrinsic mismatch between the continuous time period $\bbperiod$ and any positive integer multiple of the sampling time $\tau_s$ prevents the possibility of covering exactly one period of the reference input with an integer number of discrete-time samples. This in turn implies that the correction term $\delta$ in \eqref{eq:sdall_ssoptpred} exhibits a persistent oscillation for which $\psi$ cannot converge to $\psi^{\star}$. As a further consequence, also the cost functional \eqref{eq:sdall_ctcost} would not settle to a constant value, oscillating instead around the minimal steady-state cost. Finally, note that $K$ can still be chosen according to
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:sdall_krule}
    K = \begin{cases}
            \left\lfloor \frac{\bbperiod}{\tau_s} \right\rfloor, & \text{if $\frac{\bbperiod}{\tau_s} \leq \frac{1}{2}$},\\
            \left\lceil \frac{\bbperiod}{\tau_s} \right\rceil, & \text{otherwise},
        \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  in order to reduce the amplitude of such oscillations, as illustrated in the following.\closeremark
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:sdall_ximtrans}
  In \eqref{eq:sdall_ssopt}, the update law proposed for $\psi$ effectively steers it towards the optimal trajectory $\psi^{\star}$, as the proof of \Cref{thm:sdall_main} shows, without any assumption about the initial states of the optimizer unit. In practical applications, however, it might be desirable to start updating $\psi$ only after the buffer $\zeta$ has been filled with meaningful samples of the reference input $r$, \emph{i.e.}
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:sdall_switchedxim}
    \psi_{k+1} = \begin{cases}
                    A_{IM}\psi_k + F_{\zeta}\zeta_k + F_{IM}\psi_k, & \text{if $k \geq \bbexostatedim$},\\
                    A_{IM}\psi_k, & \text{otherwise},
                  \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  to prevent potentially unwanted transients of the optimizer from being added to the control input.\closeremark
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:sdall_alp}
  In \eqref{eq:sdall_ssopt}, after $\zeta$ has been filled with $\bbexostatedim$ samples of $\bbref$, only one state update is necessary for $\psi$ to reach $\psi^{\star}$. In practical applications, one might prefer a softer allocation action trading more time steps necessary to reach the optimal trajectory for a less intense allocator output. A possible alternative implementation is
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_ximalp}
    \psi_{k+1} &= A_{IM}\psi_k + \alpha(F_{\zeta}\zeta_k + F_{IM}\psi_k), & k\in\Z_{\geq 0},
  \end{align}
  where $\alpha\in(0,1)$.\closeremark
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:sdall_buf}
  In \eqref{eq:sdall_zeta}, the buffer $\zeta$ is designed to store $\bbexostatedim$ samples of the reference input, namely as many as the order of the exosystem. In practice, $\bar{k}<\bbexostatedim$ may be enough, where $\bar{k}\in\N$ is the smallest integer such that $O_{\bar{k}}(\exooutmat,\dexodynmat)$ has full column rank.\closeremark
\end{remark}

\begin{example}
  \label{ex:sdall}
  Consider the following state-space representation for system \eqref{eq:sdall_ctsys}:
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_ex_ctsys}
    \plantdynmat &= \begin{bmatrix}
      -3 & 2 & -9\\
      9 & -7 & 8\\
      5 & -7 & 2\end{bmatrix}, & \plantctrmat &= \begin{bmatrix}
      4 & 7\\
      -10 & -6\\
      10 & -7\end{bmatrix}, & \plantoutmat &= \begin{bmatrix}
      -7 & -4 & 1\end{bmatrix}.
  \end{align}
  In this framework, the objective of the preexisting feedback control system consists in tracking the sinusoidal reference input $r(t)$ with the plant output $y(t)$. The reference input has $h=2$ frequency components and is generated by an exosystem of the form \eqref{eq:sdall_ctexo}, which satisfies \Cref{ass:sdall_exo} with
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_ex_ctexodata}
    \bbperiod &= \frac{2\pi}{\omega_1} = \SI{1}{\second}, & \omega_2 &= \frac{\omega_1}{2}, & \exooutmat &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}, & \bbexostate(0) &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 5 & 0 & 2\end{bmatrix}'.
  \end{align}
  An inspection of \eqref{eq:sdall_ex_ctsys} reveals that it satisfies \Cref{ass:bboveract} with $\bbreddeg=1$ and that the pair $(\plantdynmat,\plantctrmat)$ is controllable, as is the pair $(\plantdynmat,{\plantctrmat}_{\left[:,1\right]})$ that is relative to the first input component alone. Thus, the regulator is designed to act only on the first input, setting the second to zero to better highlight the allocation action. The sampling time is $\tau_s=\SI{0.1}{\second}$. The regulator solves the error-feedback regulation problem applying the Internal Model Principle \cite{francis_internal_1975}, ensuring that the closed-loop system is asymptotically stable and that the steady-state tracking error is equal to zero at the sampling instants. The regulator is of the form given in \eqref{eq:sdall_dtcontroller} and, together with the plant \eqref{eq:sdall_ex_ctsys}, satisfies \Cref{ass:sdall_controller}. The discrete-time annihilator \eqref{eq:sdall_annss} is designed as stated in \Cref{prop:sdall_annih_computation}. The parameters of the steady-state optimizer \eqref{eq:sdall_ssopt} are
  \begin{align}
    \label{eq:sdall_ex_ssopt}
    R &= \begin{bmatrix}10 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}, & C_{IM} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}, & K &= \frac{\bbperiod}{\tau_s} = 10,
