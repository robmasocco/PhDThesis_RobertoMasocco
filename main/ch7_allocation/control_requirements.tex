\section{Control requirements}
\label{sec:all_prbs}
As the literature review offered in \Cref{sec:sota_allocation} points out, the current landscape of dynamic control allocation problems and proposed results is quite wide. A dynamic control allocator for the TCV shape controller should be designed to integrate within the existing framework, not only in terms of its implementation but also of the the modeling techniques on which it is based.

For this work, the following formulations of a dynamic control allocation problem have been considered. Their evaluation has led to theoretical results that are illustrated in the following and allowed the author to identify the most suitable approach for the TCV case.

\subsection{Notation}
\label{Sec:notation}
The set $\N$ is defined as containing all the positive integer numbers, excluding $0$.
Given a complex number $z\in\C$, $\Re(z)$ and $\Im(z)$ denote its real and imaginary parts, respectively.
Let $\R_{\geq0}=\{\textbf{a} \in \R:a\geq0\}$, $\Z_{\geq0}=\{\textbf{k} \in \Z:k\geq0\}$, $\C_0=\{\textbf{z} \in \C:\Re(\textbf{z})=0\}$, $\C_{<0}=\{\textbf{z} \in \C:\Re(\textbf{z})<0\}$,	and $\C_{\geq0}=\{\textbf{z} \in \C:\Re(\textbf{z})\geq0\}$.

Given a vector $v\in\R^n$, the symbol $\left\Vert v \right\Vert_2$ denotes its Euclidean norm.
Given a vector space $V$, $\dim(V)$ denotes its dimension.
$e_j$ denotes the $j$-th vector of the canonical basis of	$\R^n$.
The expression $\|v\|^{2}_R$ denotes the product $v'Rv$.

Given a matrix $A$, $\Imag(A)$ denotes its image, $\ker(A)$ denotes its null space, $A'$ denotes its transpose,  $A^{\dagger}$ denotes its Moore-Penrose inverse, and $A^\perp$ denotes a basis matrix for its null space. $\spec(A)$ denotes the spectrum of the matrix $A$.
Given two matrices $A,B\in\R^{m \times n}$ $A \odot B$ denotes their Hadamard, or entrywise, product, while $A \otimes B$ denotes their Kronecker product.
Given the square matrices $\{A_1,\dotsc,A_n\}$, the operator $\diag(A_1,\dotsc,A_n)$ denotes the square matrix obtained by concatenating the former matrices on its diagonal.

The symbol $I_n$ denotes the identity matrix of order $n$, the subscript is omitted when clear from the context.
For a block partitioned matrix $\mathcal{M}$,	$\mathcal{M}_{[k,h]}$ is its $(k,h)-$th block, $\mathcal{M}_{[:,h]}$ is its $h-$th block column, and $\mathcal{M}_{[:,1:h]}$ is the sub-matrix formed by its first $h$ block columns.

$\mathcal{L}_2([a,b])$, $a,b\in\R$, denotes the set of all the functions that are square-integrable over the interval $[a,b]$.

For $T\in\R_{>0}$ and $t\in\R$, the notation $\Mod(t, T)$ indicates the value of $t$ modulo $T$.

\subsection{Preliminary definitions}
\label{sec:defs}
It is instrumental to begin this discussion with a few preliminary definitions and models that are used throughout the chapter. Let $t\in\R_{\geq 0}$ be the time variable. Consider a continuous-time, time-invariant dynamic system $\bbsys$ described by the equations
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:bbsys}
  \bbsys := \left\{
  \begin{aligned}
    \dot{\bbstate} & = f(\bbstate, \bbref) + g(\bbstate)\bbaux\,, \\
    \bboutp     & = \bboutmapp(\bbstate, \bbaux)\,,       \\
    \bbouto     & = \bboutmapo(\bbstate)\,,
  \end{aligned}
  \right.
\end{equation}
where $\bbstate(t)\in\R^\bbstatedim$ denotes the state of the system, $\bbref(t)\in\R^{\bbrefdim}$ is an exogenous input, $\bbaux(t)\in\R^\bbauxdim$ is an \emph{auxiliary input}, and $\bboutp(t)\in\R^{\bboutpdim}$, $\bbouto(t)\in\R^{\bboutodim}$ are outputs. Let one assume that the vector fields $\bbvecf : R^{\bbstatedim} \times \R^{\bbrefdim} \rightarrow \R^\bbstatedim$ and $\bbinmap_i : \R^{\bbstatedim} \rightarrow \R^{\bbstatedim}$, $i = 1, ..., m$ and the mappings $\bboutmapp : \R^{\bbstatedim} \times \R^{\bbauxdim} \rightarrow \R^{\bboutodim}$ and $\bboutmapo : \R^{\bbstatedim} \rightarrow \R^{\bboutodim}$ are sufficiently smooth. The system \eqref{eq:bbsys} denotes a \emph{black-box model} with additional input channels that represents many situations: the ones that are of interest in the context of this work are those where plants with a preexisting control system are embedded in a single model of the form of $\bbsys$. These situations are further detailed in the following together with the peculiarities of each case that has been considered. Regarding the outputs, hereby partitioned in two vectors for convenience, $\bboutp$ is a \emph{performance output} that measures the overall performance of the system $\bbsys$ and $\bbouto$ is a \emph{regulated output}, which follows a desired behavior by the action of some component that is internal to $\bbsys$.

The exogenous input $\bbref$ is generated by the Poisson stable (see, \emph{e.g.}, \cite{isidori_nonlinear_1995} for a definition of Poisson stability) \emph{exosystem}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:bbexosys}
  \bbexo := \left\{
  \begin{aligned}
    \dot{\bbexostate} & = \bbexovecf(\bbexostate)\,,  \\
    \bbref            & = \bbexooutmap(\bbexostate)\,,
  \end{aligned}
  \right.
\end{equation}
where $\bbexostate(t)$ is the exosystem state, $\bbexostate(t) \in \bbexostateset \subset \R^{\bbexostatedim}$, $\bbexostateset$ compact invariant set, and the vector field $\bbexovecf : \R^{\bbexostatedim} \rightarrow \R^{\bbexostatedim}$ and the mapping $\bbexooutmap : \R^{\bbexostatedim} \rightarrow \R^{\bbrefdim}$ are smooth.

The interconnection of the system $\bbsys$ in eq. \eqref{eq:bbsys} with the exosystem $\bbexo$ in eq.\eqref{eq:bbexosys} is depicted in \Cref{fig:bbsys}. This model is the starting point for the analysis of the dynamic control allocation problems that follows in the rest of this chapter: each of the formulations that have been considered is a specific case that is modeled by \eqref{eq:bbsys} and \eqref{eq:bbexosys}.

The model described by the equations \eqref{eq:bbsys}-\eqref{eq:bbexosys} is further characterized by the following assumptions. The first one is instrumental to enable the formulation of the control allocation problems and the design of dynamic allocators.
\begin{assumption}
  \label{ass:bboveract}
  The system $\bbsys$ is \emph{over-actuated} with respect to the auxiliary input $\bbaux$ and the regulated output $\bbouto$, \emph{i.e.}, $\bbauxdim>\bboutodim$.\closestatement
\end{assumption}

The second assumption characterizes the black-box model $\bbsys$ as a preexisting dynamic system whose auxiliary input channel can be exploited to improve the performance indicator.
\begin{assumption}
  \label{ass:bbiss}
  The system $\bbsys$ is input-to-state stable (ISS) with respect to the input $(\bbref,\,\bbaux)$. Moreover, the equilibrium $\bbstate=0$ of the system $\dot{\bbstate} = \bbvecf(\bbstate,0)$ is locally asymptotically stable in the first approximation.\closestatement
\end{assumption}

Finally, the third assumption clarifies the role of the exosystem $\bbexo$ in the considered classes of problems.
\begin{assumption}
  \label{ass:bbssresp}
  The exogenous input $r$, generated by the exosystem given in eq. \eqref{eq:bbexosys}, is periodic of period $\bbperiod$.
  Moreover, there exists a mapping $\fbimap : \bbexostateset^\circ \rightarrow \R^\bbstatedim$, where $\bbexostateset^\circ\subset\bbexostateset$ is a neighborhood of the origin of $\,\R^{\bbexostatedim}$, which, for all $\bbexostate\in\bbexostateset^{\circ}$, satisfies
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:bbfbi}
    \frac{\partial\fbimap}{\partial\bbexostate}\bbexovecf(\bbexostate) = \bbvecf(\fbimap(\bbexostate), \bbexooutmap(\bbexostate))\,.
  \end{equation}
  \closestatement
\end{assumption}
It is worth to point out that \Cref{ass:bbssresp} provides, in this context, the definition of a \emph{steady-state response} of the system described by eq. \eqref{eq:bbsys} (see \cite[Prop. 8.1.1]{isidori_nonlinear_1995}).
Moreover, provided that \Cref{ass:bbiss} also holds, such a steady-state response is given by $\bbssstate(t)=\fbimap(\bbexostate(t))$ for any $t \geqslant 0$, which is therefore also periodic of period $\bbperiod$; subsequently, the performance and regulated outputs also admit steady-state responses denoted by $\bbssoutp(\cdot)$ and $\bbssouto(\cdot)$, respectively.

Let $\tau_0\in\R$. The performance criteria with respect to which the behavior of $\bbsys$ may be optimized are expressed by the following \emph{Lagrange functional} of the form
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:bbcost}
  J(\bbaux) = \int_{\tau_0}^{\tau_0 + \bbperiod} \bbcost(\bbssoutp(\tau))\,d\tau\,,
\end{equation}
where the function $\bbcost : \R^{\bboutpdim} \rightarrow \R_{\geqslant 0}$ is continuously differentiable, convex, and radially unbounded.

The two dynamic control allocation problems that are of interest for this work are formalized as follows. Recalling eq. \eqref{eq:bbsys}, the allocation action is implemented by means of the auxiliary input channel $\bbaux$ of the system $\bbsys$. Thus, the nature of the following two problems derives from two different ways in which the effect of the auxiliary input on the regulated output can be evaluated.

\begin{problem}[Dynamic allocation with steady-state output invisibility]
  \label{prb:ssinv}
  Consider the system $\bbsys$ in eq. \eqref{eq:bbsys} and the exosystem $\bbexo$ in eq. \eqref{eq:bbexosys}. Suppose that \Cref{ass:bboveract,ass:bbiss,ass:bbssresp} hold.
  Design, if possible, an input allocation device (namely, an \emph{allocator}) having input $\allin$ and output $\allout$ such that, when interconnected to $\bbsys$ and $\bbexo$ according to
	\begin{align*}
		\allin  & = \bbexostate\,, \\
		\allout & = \bbaux\,,
	\end{align*}
  ensures that the following requirements are met.
  \begin{itemize}
		\item[\emph{(P)}] \emph{Steady-state periodicity:} the steady-state output response of the allocator, namely $\allssout(\cdot)$, is periodic of period $\bbperiod$.
		\item[\emph{(I)}] \emph{Steady-state output invisibility:} the steady-state regulated output responses of the system given by eq. \eqref{eq:bbsys} both in presence and in absence of the allocation action, namely $\bbssoutoa(\cdot)$ and $\bbssouto(\cdot)$ respectively, are such that
		  \begin{align}
		    \label{eq:ssinv}
		    \bbssoutoa(t) = \bbssouto(t)
		  \end{align}
		  for all $t\in\R_{\geqslant 0}\,$.
		\item[\emph{(O)}] \emph{Steady-state optimality:} the cost functional $J$ in eq. \eqref{eq:bbcost} is minimized.
    \end{itemize}
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Dynamic allocation with full output invisibility]
  \label{prb:fullinv}
  Consider the system $\bbsys$ in eq. \eqref{eq:bbsys} and the exosystem $\bbexo$ in eq. \eqref{eq:bbexosys}. Suppose that \Cref{ass:bboveract,ass:bbiss,ass:bbssresp} hold.
  Design, if possible, an input allocation device (namely, an \emph{allocator}) having input $\allin$ and output $\allout$ such that, when interconnected to $\bbsys$ and $\bbexo$ according to
	\begin{align*}
		\allin  & = \bbexostate\,, \\
		\allout & = \bbaux\,,
	\end{align*}
  ensures that the following requirements are met.
  \begin{itemize}
		\item[\emph{(P)}] \emph{Steady-state periodicity:} the steady-state output response of the allocator, namely $\allssout(\cdot)$, is periodic of period $\bbperiod$.
		\item[\emph{(I)}] \emph{Full output invisibility:} the regulated output responses of the system given by eq. \eqref{eq:bbsys} both in presence and in absence of the allocation action, namely $\bboutoa(\cdot)$ and $\bbouto(\cdot)$ respectively, are such that
		  \begin{align}
		    \label{eq:ssinv}
		    \bboutoa(t) = \bbouto(t)
		  \end{align}
		  for all $t\in\R_{\geqslant 0}\,$.
		\item[\emph{(O)}] \emph{Steady-state optimality:} the cost functional $J$ in eq. \eqref{eq:bbcost} is minimized.
    \end{itemize}
\end{problem}

It is finally worth to point out that, in both problems, the optimization process is allowed to exhibit a transient behavior: what effectively distinguishes the two situations is whether the effect of this transient behavior on the regulated output can be visible or not. The results that follow in the rest of this chapter provide solutions to \Cref{prb:ssinv,prb:fullinv} based on different configurations of the model given in eq. \eqref{eq:bbsys}-\eqref{eq:bbexosys}. The case of \Cref{prb:ssinv} is examined considering both a linear and a nonlinear model for the system $\bbsys$ in eq. \eqref{eq:bbsys}. On the other hand, \Cref{prb:fullinv} is studied from the only point of view of linear systems but in multiple scenarios: when the model in eq. \eqref{eq:bbsys} is fully known, when it is affected by parametric uncertainties, and when it includes sampled data devices.
