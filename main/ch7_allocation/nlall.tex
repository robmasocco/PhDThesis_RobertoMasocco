The nonlinear case is now addressed. Consider the dynamic system \eqref{eq:bbsys}, subject to the reference input produced by the exosystem \eqref{eq:bbexosys}. Suppose that \Cref{ass:bboveract,ass:bbiss,ass:bbssresp} hold and consider the steady-state cost functional \eqref{eq:bbcost}, which evaluates the steady-state performance output of the system \eqref{eq:bbsys} subject to the allocation action of the auxiliary input $\bbaux$. In a similar way to the previous linear case, let $\ell(\bbssoutp(t))=\left\Vert\bbssoutp(t)\right\Vert^2_2$ and $\tau_0=0$ in \eqref{eq:bbcost}, yielding
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:nlall_sscost}
  J(\bbaux) = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{\bbperiod} \left\Vert\bbssoutp(\tau)\right\Vert^2_2\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
In this scenario, as in many others reported in the literature (see \Cref{sec:sota_allocation}), the most cumbersome requirement set by \Cref{prb:ssinv} is the output invisibility statement (I), which, in the previous linear case of \Cref{prb:lqrall_optcont}, was still included as a problem constraint. Conversely, the approach hereby proposed for the original formulation of \Cref{prb:ssinv} involving \eqref{eq:bbsys}-\eqref{eq:bbexosys} stems from a different reasoning: \Cref{prb:ssinv} is reformulated as an optimal control problem that approximates the original one by penalizing the steady-state output invisibility requirement in the cost functional \cite{masocco_dynamic_2024}. Then, a numerical algorithm is developed to determine candidate solutions to the approximated problem.

To substantiate this evidence few preliminary definitions are in order. Let $\bbstate_N(t)$, $\bbstate_N(t)\in\R^\bbstatedim$ denote the state vector of \eqref{eq:bbsys} at time $t$ in the absence or presence of the allocator, respectively, that is
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:nlall_xnxa}
  \begin{align}
    \dot{\bbstate}_N(t) & = \bbvecf(\bbstate_N(t), \bbref(t)), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\label{eq:nlall_xn} \\
    \dot{\bbstate}_A(t) & = \bbvecf(\bbstate_A(t), \bbref(t)) + \bbinmap(\bbstate_A(t))\bbaux(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\label{eq:nlall_xa}
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}
and let $\bbssstate_{N},\,\bbssstate_{A}$ denote the corresponding steady-state responses, provided that the latter exists. Define the \emph{output invisibility function} $h_I(\bbstate_N, \bbstate_A) : \R^\bbstatedim\times\R^\bbstatedim \rightarrow \R$ in the following way:
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:nlall_hi}
  h_I(\bbstate_N(t), \bbstate_A(t)) &= \frac{1}{2} \left\Vert \bboutmapo(\bbstate_N(t)) - \bboutmapo(\bbstate_A(t)) \right\Vert_2^2, & t\in\R_{\geq 0}.
\end{align}
A relaxed formulation of \Cref{prb:ssinv} is the following constrained optimal control problem.

\begin{problem}[Optimal input allocation for nonlinear dynamics]
  \label{prb:nlall_optcont}
  Consider the system \eqref{eq:bbsys} and suppose that \Cref{ass:bboveract,ass:bbiss,ass:bbssresp} hold. Given $\bbexostate_0\in\bbexostateset^{\circ}$, let $\bbexostate(0) = \bbexostate_0$. Let $h_I(\bbstate_N(t),\bbstate_A(t))$ be defined as in \eqref{eq:nlall_hi}. Let $v : \R \rightarrow \R^\bbauxdim$ and define the cost functionals
  \begin{subequations}
    \label{eq:nlall_Js}
    \begin{align}
      J_p(v) & = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^\bbperiod \left\Vert \bboutmapp(\bbssstate_{A}(t), v(t)) \right\Vert_2^2\,dt,\label{eq:nlall_Jp} \\
      J_I(v) & = \int_0^\bbperiod h_I(\bbssstate_{N}(t),\bbssstate_{A}(t))\,dt.\label{eq:nlall_Ji}
    \end{align}
  \end{subequations}
  Determine, if any, a function $v^\star\in\mathcal{L}_2([0, \bbperiod])$, periodic of period $\bbperiod$, such that the cost functional
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:nlall_J}
    J(v) = J_p(v) + \alpha J_I(v),
  \end{equation}
  with a fixed $\alpha\in\R_{>0}$, is brought to its minimum value, satisfying the differential constraints \eqref{eq:nlall_xn}-\eqref{eq:nlall_xa} with $\bbaux=v$.\closestatement
\end{problem}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:nlall_prbrel}
  The output invisibility requirement, \emph{i.e.}, statement (I) of \Cref{prb:ssinv}, represents the main challenge of dynamic input allocation problems in general. In \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont}, by \eqref{eq:nlall_Ji} and \eqref{eq:nlall_J}, this requirement is included as part of the minimization objective, implying that the solutions to \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont} can only approximate the behavior of those to \Cref{prb:ssinv}. It is reasonable to expect that the quality of the approximation depends on the specific solutions to \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont} that can be determined, using numerical methods, as $\alpha\rightarrow +\infty$.\closeremark
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:nlall_ssua}
  Although a generic allocator that solves \Cref{prb:ssinv} may generally exhibit transients in its output $\allout$, the solution to \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont} consists of a steady-state behavior repeated over the time interval $[0,\bbperiod]$, similar to the previous linear case \cite{galeani_optimal_2023}. However, there may still be transients in the overall system response of \eqref{eq:bbsys} and in the remaining signals as they converge to their steady-state, allocated behavior.\closeremark
\end{remark}

Let $\lambda_N(t),\lambda_A(t)\in\R^{\bbstatedim}$ be the costate variables, associated to the nominal (\emph{i.e.}, non-allocated) and allocated state variables, respectively. In the following, time dependencies are omitted to ease the notation. Recalling \eqref{eq:nlall_xnxa}--\eqref{eq:nlall_J}, define the Hamiltonian function
\begin{multline}
  \label{eq:nlall_H}
  H(\bbstate_N(t),\bbstate_A(t),\lambda_N(t),\lambda_A(t),v(t)) \\
  =\frac{1}{2}\left\Vert \bboutmapp(\bbstate_A(t),v(t)) \right\Vert_2^2 + \alpha h_I(\bbstate_N(t),\bbstate_A(t)) + \lambda_N(t)'\bbvecf(\bbstate_N(t),\bbref(t)) \\
  + \lambda_A(t)'(\bbvecf(\bbstate_A(t),\bbref(t)) + \bbinmap(\bbstate_A(t))v(t)), \qquad t\in\R_{\geq 0}.
\end{multline}
Consider (omitting, for the sake of simplicity, some dependencies)
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:nlall_dHdv}
  H_v = \bboutmapp(\bbstate_A,v)'\frac{\partial \bboutmapp}{\partial v} + \lambda_A'\bbinmap(\bbstate_A),
\end{equation}
and note that the costate dynamics are given by
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:nlall_lambdadot}
  \begin{align}
    \dot{\lambda}_N(t) &= -\alpha\left.\frac{\partial h_I(\bbstate_N,\bbstate_A)}{\partial \bbstate_N}'\right|_{\bbstate_N=\bbstate_N(t),\bbstate_A=\bbstate_A(t)}\notag\\
    &- \left.\frac{\partial \bbvecf(\bbstate_N,\bbref)}{\partial \bbstate_N}'\right|_{\bbstate_N=\bbstate_N(t),\bbref=\bbref(t)}\lambda_N(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\label{eq:nlal_lambdandot}\\
    \dot{\lambda}_A(t) &= -\frac{\partial \bboutmapp(\bbstate_A(t),v(t))}{\partial \bbstate_A}'\bboutmapp(\bbstate_A(t),v(t))\notag\\
    &- \alpha\left.\frac{\partial h_I(\bbstate_N,\bbstate_A)}{\partial \bbstate_A}'\right|_{\bbstate_N=\bbstate_N(t),\bbstate_A=\bbstate_A(t)} - \left.\frac{\partial \bbvecf(\bbstate_A,\bbref)}{\partial \bbstate_A}'\right|_{\bbstate_A=\bbstate_A(t),\bbref=\bbref(t)}\lambda_A(t)\notag\\
    &- \sum_{i=1}^{\bbauxdim}v_i(t)\left.\frac{\partial \bbinmap_i(\bbstate_A)}{\partial \bbstate_A}'\right|_{\bbstate_A=\bbstate_A(t)}\lambda_A(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\label{eq:nlall_lambdaadot}
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $v_i$ and $\bbinmap_i$ denote the $i$-th entry and column of $v$ and $\bbinmap$, respectively.
Given the function $u$ with values $u(t)\in\R^\bbauxdim$, the periodic extension of $u$ is denoted by $\hat{u} : \R \rightarrow \R^\bbauxdim$, \emph{i.e.}, $\hat{u}(t) = u(\Mod(t, \bbperiod))$.
Let $\beta,\gamma\in\R_{>0}$, and $\bbstate_N$ be the solution of \eqref{eq:nlall_xn}. Fix a final time $t_f=a\bbperiod$ with $a\in\N$ large enough to include a ``reasonable'' number of periods, an arbitrary $v_0$ with values in $v_0(t)\in\R^\bbauxdim$, fix $(\lambda_N(t_f),\lambda_A(t_f))=(0,0)$, and define
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:nlall_deltadH}
  H_v^\delta(\bbstate_A,\lambda_A,v) = \left( \int_{t_f-\bbperiod}^{t_f} H_v(\bbstate_A(t),\lambda_A(t),v(t)) H_v(\bbstate_A(t),\lambda_A(t),v(t))'\,dt \right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The proposed method is stated in \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep}, and its properties are characterized by the following theorem.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
  \caption{Steady-state steepest descent algorithm \cite{masocco_dynamic_2024}.}
  \label{alg:nlall_sweep}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require $\gamma,\,\bbperiod,\,t_f,\,[0,t_f],\,v_0,\,\bbstate_N,\,(\lambda_N(t_f),\lambda_A(t_f)),\,\bbstate_A(0)$
    \Ensure $\tilde{v}$
    \State $\tilde{v}_0 \gets v_0$
    \For{$k=1,\dotsc$}
      \State $\bbstate_{A,k} \gets$ the solution of \eqref{eq:nlall_xa} with $\bbaux=\tilde{v}_{k-1}$, ${\bbstate_A}_0=\bbstate_A(0)$
      \State $(\lambda_{N,k},\lambda_{A,k}) \gets$ backward integration of \eqref{eq:nlall_lambdadot} with
      \Statex \hspace{3.05cm}$(\bbstate_A,v)=(\bbstate_{A,k},\tilde{v}_{k-1})$, $(\lambda_N(t_f),\lambda_A(t_f))$ given
      \If{$H_v^\delta(\bbstate_{A,k},\lambda_{A,k},\tilde{v}_{k-1}) = 0$}
        \Return $\tilde{v} \gets \tilde{v}_{k-1}$
      \Else
        \State $u_k \gets \tilde{v}_{k-1} - \gamma\,H_v(\bbstate_{A,k},\lambda_{A,k},\tilde{v}_{k-1})$
        \State $\tilde{v}_k \gets \hat{\tilde{u}}_k$ \Comment{Periodic extension of the steady-state.}
      \EndIf
    \EndFor
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}
  \label{thm:nlall_main}
  Consider system \eqref{eq:bbsys}, fix a large enough $\alpha>0$, and suppose that \Cref{ass:bboveract,ass:bbiss,ass:bbssresp} hold. Given $v_0 : \R \rightarrow \R^{\bbauxdim}$, and $t_f=a\bbperiod$, $a\in\N$, there exists a sequence of updates $\{\Delta v_k\}_{k=1}^h$, $\Delta v_k\in\R^\bbauxdim$, $h\in\N$, such that \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} terminates in $h$ steps yielding the sequence $\{\tilde{v}_k\}_{k=1}^h$ that converges to an extremal solution of \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont}.\closestatement
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  Given the standing assumptions and hypotheses, the existence of the sequences $\{\Delta v_k\}_{k=1}^h$ and $\{\tilde{v}_k\}_{k=1}^h$, as well as their convergence properties, are guaranteed by the Steepest Descent Algorithm (see \cite[Sec. 6.2]{kirk_optimal_1970} and references therein), properly adapted as in \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} to face \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:nlall_algparams}
  In practical scenarios, a numerical stopping criterion evaluating the norm of the gradient at each step is put in place, trading asymptotic convergence for implementation simplicity. One possible example is, for a fixed $\beta\in\R_{>0}$, evaluating
  \begin{align}
    H_v^\delta(\bbstate_{A,k},\lambda_{A,k},\tilde{v}_{k-1}) &\leq \beta, & k\in\N,
  \end{align}
  instead of
  \begin{align}
    H_v^\delta(\bbstate_{A,k},\lambda_{A,k},\tilde{v}_{k-1}) &= 0, & k\in\N.
  \end{align}
  Regarding the update step $\gamma$, it is selected in principle as a constant value. Consequently, if the magnitude of the update step is large the algorithm may not converge.
  Furthermore, the feasibility of \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} may also depend on the value of the parameter $\alpha$. Recalling \eqref{eq:nlall_lambdaadot}, a large value made the backward integration numerically unfeasible in the simulations that have been performed to test this method.\closeremark
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:nlall_dt}
  Note that \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} is purely methodological: in practical scenarios, numerical integrations over the considered time interval $[0,t_f]$ are necessary, adding multiple layers of configurable parameters to the algorithm. First, the choice of the numerical algorithm, \emph{e.g.}, \emph{Runge-Kutta} methods, is crucial to ensure the convergence of the backward integration of \eqref{eq:nlall_lambdadot}. Moreover, since discretizations are involved, a key aspect to consider is also the interpolation method to obtain all the signals from computed samples. Finally, another parameter of interest concerning the numerical implementation of \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} is the sampling interval with respect to which the time interval $[0,t_f]$ is discretized. The magnitude of the sampling interval must usually be small to enable a feasible approximation of the continuous-time dynamics involved in \Cref{prb:ssinv} and has direct repercussions on the computational complexity of the integration steps.\closeremark
\end{remark}

The choice of the update step in \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} is a crucial design task. Many heuristic techniques have emerged in the literature to improve the convergence of this method, in particular thanks to its extensive diffusion in finite-dimensional optimization fields such as, \emph{e.g.}, machine learning for the training of neural networks \cite{goodfellow_deep_2016}.

The ADAM scheme yields the update rule given in \Cref{alg:nlall_adam}. With an abuse of notation, the variables $s$ and $r$ store at each iteration, respectively, $H_v$ and $H_v \odot H_v$ of the current and of the previous iterations. Note also that the operation $(\cdot)^{1/2}$ on $\hat{r}$, as well as the operation $(\cdot)^{-1}$ on $(\hat{r}^{1/2}+\delta)$, reported on line 14 of \Cref{alg:nlall_adam}, are both performed component-wise. The values $\rho_1$ for $s$, and $\rho_2$ for $r$, constitute a measure of the importance of the past history, which is then used for the proper evaluation of the update step. In particular, an appropriate choice of these parameters allows one to discard all the information related to previous iterations, thus improving the update step by relying only on the most recent information. It is worth mentioning that this scheme adjusts the update step dynamically during the iterations, differently from the scheme provided by \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep}. The observations made in \Cref{rem:nlall_dt} continue to hold also for \Cref{alg:nlall_adam}.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
	\caption{Steady-state ADAM algorithm \cite{masocco_dynamic_2024}.}
	\label{alg:nlall_adam}
	\begin{algorithmic}[1]
		\Require $\gamma,\,\bbperiod,\,t_f,\,[0,t_f],\,v_0,\,\bbstate_N,\,(\lambda_N(t_f),\lambda_A(t_f)),\,\bbstate_A(0),\,\rho_1,\,\rho_2,\,\delta$
		\Ensure $\tilde{v}$
		\State $\tilde{v}_0 \gets v_0$
		\State $s_0\gets0$
		\State $r_0\gets0$
		\For{$k=1,\dotsc$}
			\State $\bbstate_{A,k} \gets$ the solution of \eqref{eq:nlall_xa} with $\bbaux=\tilde{v}_{k-1}$, ${\bbstate_A}_0=\bbstate_A(0)$
			\State $(\lambda_{N,k},\lambda_{A,k}) \gets$ backward integration of \eqref{eq:nlall_lambdadot} with
      \Statex \hspace{3.05cm}$(\bbstate_A,v)=(\bbstate_{A,k},\tilde{v}_{k-1})$, $(\lambda_N(t_f),\lambda_A(t_f))$ given
      \If{$H_v^\delta(\bbstate_{A,k},\lambda_{A,k},\tilde{v}_{k-1}) = 0$}
        \Return $\tilde{v} \gets \tilde{v}_{k-1}$
			\Else
				\State $H_v \gets H_v(\bbstate_{A,k},\lambda_{A,k},\tilde{v}_{k-1})$
				\State $s_k\gets\rho_1s_{k-1}+(1-\rho_1)H_v$
				\State $r_k\gets\rho_2r_{k-1}+(1-\rho_2)H_v \odot H_v$
				\State $\hat{s} \gets s/(1-\rho_{1,k})$
				\State $\hat{r} \gets r/(1-\rho_{2,k})$
				\State $\Delta u_k\gets-\gamma\,\hat{s}\odot((\hat{r})^{1/2}+\delta)^{-1}$
				\State $u_k \gets \tilde{v}_{k-1} + \Delta u_{k} $
				\State $\tilde{v}_{k} \gets \hat{\tilde{u}}_k$ \Comment{Periodic extension of the steady-state.}
			\EndIf
		\EndFor
	\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{example}
  Consider the following continuous-time exosystem of the form \eqref{eq:bbexosys}:
  \begin{subequations}
    \label{eq:nlall_simexo}
    \begin{align}
      \dot{\bbexostate}(t) &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0\\ -\omega^2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \bbexostate(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\\
      \bbref(t) &= \begin{bmatrix} \bbexostate_1(t) + \bbexostate_3(t) \\ \bbexostate_2(t) \end{bmatrix}, & t\in\R_{\geq 0},
    \end{align}
  \end{subequations}
  with $\omega=2\pi f$, $f=\SI{0.2}{\hertz}$, and $\bbexostate_0 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \omega & 2 \end{bmatrix}'$. Hence, consider the following continuous-time system of the form \eqref{eq:bbsys}:
  \begin{subequations}
    \label{eq:nlall_simplant}
    \begin{align}
      \dot{\bbstate}(t) &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \bbstate_1(t)+\bbstate_2(t) \\ \bbstate_1(t)-\bbstate_2(t) \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} e^{\bbstate_2(t)} & 1 \\ e^{\bbstate_2(t)} & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} (h_c(\bbstate(t),r(t))+\bbaux(t)), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\\
      \bboutp(t) &= h_c(\bbstate(t),\bbref(t)) + \bbaux(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\\
      \bbouto(t) &= (1+\bbstate_3(t))^3, & t\in\R_{\geq 0},
    \end{align}
  \end{subequations}
  where $h_c : \R^3\times\R^3 \rightarrow \R^2$ is a given feedback control law, and $\bbaux : \R \rightarrow \R^2$ is the auxiliary input, to be determined as an extremal solution to \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont}.

  The control objective in this example is that of tracking, with the regulated output $\bbouto$, the reference input $\bbref_1(t)=\bbexostate_1(t)+\bbexostate_3(t)=\sin(\omega t)+2$, $t\in\R_{\geq 0}$. To this end, the control law $h_c$ has been designed to first apply a \emph{linearizing feedback} to the system \eqref{eq:nlall_simplant} and then to stabilize the resulting linearized dynamics. Subsequently, the tracking objective is achieved by injecting the reference input into the feedback control law. A detailed description of the synthesis procedure is provided in \cite[Chap. 4]{isidori_nonlinear_1995}.
  To highlight the optimization action operated by the auxiliary input $\bbaux$, in a similar way to the previous linear case, the control law $h_c$ has been designed to operate only on the first input, setting the second input to zero (as described in \cite[Chap. 4]{isidori_nonlinear_1995}, this is possible because an inspection of \eqref{eq:nlall_simplant} reveals that the system has relative degree $r=3$ from the first component of the input, affecting the system via the input map $\bbinmap_1(\chi) = \begin{bmatrix} e^{\bbstate_2} & e^{\bbstate_2} & 0 \end{bmatrix}'$, to the output $\bbouto$).

  \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} has been tested with parameters $(\gamma,\,t_f) = (10^{-4}, 15)$, $v_0 = 0$, and the initial condition of \eqref{eq:nlall_simplant} given by $\bbstate(0)=\begin{bmatrix}0.1872 & -0.0767 & 0.2599\end{bmatrix}'$. All algorithms have been implemented numerically in MATLAB following the procedure described in \Cref{rem:nlall_dt}, \emph{i.e.}, by discretizing the time interval $[0,t_f]$ with a sampling interval of $10^{-2}$, performing fixed-step numerical integration of the state and costate dynamics, and operating with sampled signals.

  The stopping criterion has been disregarded intentionally, setting a maximum number of iterations equal to $10^4$. The goal of this test is twofold: first, check the performance of the algorithm, and secondly, tune the parameter $\alpha$. \Cref{fig:nlall_errs} shows the steady-state tracking errors achieved with different values of $\alpha$: the problem remains computationally feasible and it is possible to set even a large invisibility weight, \emph{i.e.}, $\alpha=10^5$, achieving a small output error.

  A comparison has been made, retaining all the parameters, between the rates of convergence of \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} and of \Cref{alg:nlall_adam}, selecting for the latter the parameters $\rho_1 = 0.9$, $\rho_2 = 0.999$ and $\delta = 10^{-8}$. \Cref{fig:nlall_cost} shows how \Cref{alg:nlall_adam} is faster than \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} in this example, converging in about half the maximum number of iterations allowed and well before the convergence of \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep}. Note that it has not been possible to make \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} achieve the same speed of convergence of \Cref{alg:nlall_adam}: increasing the constant update step $\gamma$ leads to a divergence of the algorithm.

  \Cref{fig:nlall_hcn} shows the nominal system input given by the feedback linearization and stabilization process and \Cref{fig:nlall_ua} depicts the auxiliary input determined by \Cref{alg:nlall_adam}. Finally, \Cref{fig:nlall_hca} illustrates the performance output subject to the allocator action, to be compared with the time histories in \Cref{fig:nlall_hcn}. It is worth pointing out that, even if the feedback-linearizing law uses only one input, the auxiliary one uses both. Thus, in accordance with the definition of the performance output in this example, $\bboutp$ exhibits a reduced amplitude leading to a smaller norm, all of which while only a small alteration in the regulated output $\bbouto$ is observed.\closeexample
\end{example}
