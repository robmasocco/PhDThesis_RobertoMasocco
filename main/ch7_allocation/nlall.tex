The nonlinear case is now addressed. Consider the dynamic system \eqref{eq:bbsys}, subject to the reference input produced by the exosystem \eqref{eq:bbexosys}. Suppose that \Cref{ass:bboveract,ass:bbiss,ass:bbssresp} hold and consider the steady-state cost functional \eqref{eq:bbcost}, which evaluates the steady-state performance output of the TITO model \eqref{eq:bbsys} subject to the allocation action of the auxiliary input $\bbaux$. In a similar way the previous linear case, let $\ell(\bbssoutp(t))=\left\Vert\bbssoutp(t)\right\Vert^2_2$ and $\tau_0=0$ in \eqref{eq:bbcost}, yielding
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:nlall_sscost}
  J(\bbaux) = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{\bbperiod} \left\Vert\bbssoutp(\tau)\right\Vert^2_2\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
In this scenario, as in many others reported in the literature (see \Cref{sec:sota_allocation}), the most cumbersome requirement set by \Cref{prb:ssinv} is the output invisibility item (I), which, in the previous linear case of \Cref{prb:lqrall_optcont}, was still included as a problem constraint. Conversely, the approach hereby proposed for the original formulation of \Cref{prb:ssinv} involving \eqref{eq:bbsys}-\eqref{eq:bbexosys} stems from a different reasoning: \Cref{prb:ssinv} is reformulated as an optimal control problem that approximates the original one by penalizing the steady-state output invisibility requirement in the cost functional \cite{masocco_dynamic_2024}. Then, a numerical algorithm is developed to determine candidate solutions to the approximated problem.

To substantiate this evidence few preliminary definitions are in order. Let $\bbstate_n(t)$, $\bbstate_a(t)\in\R^\bbstatedim$ denote the state vector of \eqref{eq:bbsys} at time $t$ in the absence or presence of the allocator, respectively, that is
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:nlall_xnxa}
  \begin{align}
    \dot{\bbstate}_n(t) & = \bbvecf(\bbstate_n(t), \bbref(t)), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\label{eq:nlall_xn} \\
    \dot{\bbstate}_a(t) & = \bbvecf(\bbstate_a(t), \bbref(t)) + \bbinmap(\bbstate_a(t))\bbaux(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\label{eq:nlall_xa}
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}
and let $\bbssstate_{n},\,\bbssstate_{a}$ denote the corresponding steady-state responses, provided that the latter exists. Define the \emph{output invisibility function} $h_i(\bbstate_n, \bbstate_a) : \R^\bbstatedim\times\R^\bbstatedim \rightarrow \R$ in the following way:
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:nlall_hi}
  h_i(\bbstate_n(t), \bbstate_a(t)) &= \frac{1}{2} \left\Vert \bboutmapo(\bbstate_n(t)) - \bboutmapo(\bbstate_a(t)) \right\Vert_2^2, & t\in\R_{\geq 0}.
\end{align}
A relaxed formulation of \Cref{prb:ssinv} is the following constrained optimal control problem.

\begin{problem}[Optimal input allocation for nonlinear dynamics]
  \label{prb:nlall_optcont}
  Consider the system \eqref{eq:bbsys} and suppose that \Cref{ass:bboveract,ass:bbiss,ass:bbssresp} hold. Given $\bbexostate_0\in\bbexostateset^{\circ}$, let $\bbexostate(0) = \bbexostate_0$. Let $h_i(\bbstate_n(t),\bbstate_a(t))$ be defined as in \eqref{eq:nlall_hi}. Let $v : \R \rightarrow \R^\bbauxdim$ and define the cost functionals
  \begin{subequations}
    \label{eq:nlall_Js}
    \begin{align}
      J_p(v) & = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^\bbperiod \left\Vert \bboutmapp(\bbssstate_{a}(t), v(t)) \right\Vert_2^2\,dt,\label{eq:nlall_Jp} \\
      J_i(v) & = \int_0^\bbperiod h_i(\bbssstate_{n}(t),\bbssstate_{a}(t))\,dt.\label{eq:nlall_Ji}
    \end{align}
  \end{subequations}
  Determine, if any, a function $v^\star\in\mathcal{L}_2([0, \bbperiod])$, periodic of period $\bbperiod$, such that the cost functional
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:nlall_J}
    J(v) = J_p(v) + \alpha J_i(v),
  \end{equation}
  with a fixed $\alpha\in\R_{>0}$, is brought to its minimum value, satisfying the differential constraints \eqref{eq:nlall_xn}-\eqref{eq:nlall_xa} with $\bbaux=v$.\closestatement
\end{problem}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:nlall_prbrel}
  The output invisibility requirement, \emph{i.e.}, item (I) of \Cref{prb:ssinv}, represents the main challenge of dynamic input allocation problems in general. In \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont}, by \eqref{eq:nlall_Ji} and \eqref{eq:nlall_J}, this requirement is included as part of the minimization objective, implying that the solutions to \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont} can only approximate the behavior of those to \Cref{prb:ssinv}. It is reasonable to expect that the quality of the approximation depends on the specific solutions to \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont} that can be determined, using numerical methods, as $\alpha\rightarrow +\infty$.\closeremark
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:nlall_ssua}
  Although a generic allocator that solves \Cref{prb:ssinv} may generally exhibit transients in its output $\allout$, the solution to \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont} consists of a steady-state behavior repeated over the time interval $[0,\bbperiod]$, similar to the previous linear case \cite{galeani_optimal_2023}. However, there may still be transients in the overall system response of \eqref{eq:bbsys} and in the remaining signals as they converge to their steady-state, allocated behavior.\closeremark
\end{remark}

Let $\lambda_n(t),\lambda_a(t)\in\R^{\bbstatedim}$ be the costate variables, associated to the nominal (\emph{i.e.}, non-allocated) and allocated state variables, respectively. In the following, time dependencies are omitted to ease the notation. Recalling \eqref{eq:nlall_xnxa}--\eqref{eq:nlall_J}, define the Hamiltonian function
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:nlall_H}
  \begin{split}
    &H(\bbstate_n(t),\bbstate_a(t),\lambda_n(t),\lambda_a(t),v(t)) \\
    &\hspace{-1.5pt}=\frac{1}{2}\left\Vert \bboutmapp(\bbstate_a(t),v(t)) \right\Vert_2^2 + \alpha h_i(\bbstate_n(t),\bbstate_a(t)) +\lambda_n(t)'\bbvecf(\bbstate_n(t),\bbref(t)) \\
    &\hspace{-1.5pt}+ \lambda_a(t)'(\bbvecf(\bbstate_a(t),\bbref(t)) + \bbinmap(\bbstate_a(t))v(t)), \quad t\in\R_{\geq 0}.
  \end{split}
\end{equation}
For the sake of simplicity, define
\begin{multline}
  \label{eq:nlall_dHdv}
  H_v(\bbstate_a,\lambda_a,v) = \frac{\partial H(\bbstate_n,\bbstate_a,\lambda_n,\lambda_a,v)}{\partial v}
  = \bboutmapp(\bbstate_a,v)'\frac{\partial \bboutmapp(\bbstate_a,v)}{\partial v} + \lambda_a'\bbinmap(\bbstate_a),
\end{multline}
and note that the costate dynamics are given by
\begin{subequations}
  \label{eq:nlall_lambdadot}
  \begin{align}
    \dot{\lambda}_n(t) &= -\alpha\frac{\partial h_i(\bbstate_n(t),\bbstate_a(t))}{\partial \bbstate_n}' - \frac{\partial \bbvecf(\bbstate_n(t),\bbref(t))}{\partial \bbstate_n}'\lambda_n(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\label{eq:nlal_lambdandot}\\
    \begin{split}
      \dot{\lambda}_a(t) &= -\frac{\partial \bboutmapp(\bbstate_a(t),v(t))}{\partial \bbstate_a}'\bboutmapp(\bbstate_a(t),v(t))\\
      &- \alpha\frac{\partial h_i(\bbstate_n(t),\bbstate_a(t))}{\partial \bbstate_a}' - \frac{\partial \bbvecf(\bbstate_a(t),\bbref(t))}{\partial \bbstate_a}'\lambda_a(t)\\
      &- \sum_{\iota=1}^{\bbauxdim}v_\iota(t)\frac{\partial \bbinmap_\iota(\bbstate_a(t))}{\partial \bbstate_a}'\lambda_a(t),
    \end{split}& t\in\R_{\geq 0},\label{eq:nlall_lambdaadot}
  \end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $v_\iota(t)$ and $\bbinmap_\iota(t)$ denote the $\iota$-th component and column of $v(t)$ and $\bbinmap(t)$, respectively.
Given the function $u$ with values $u(t)\in\R^\bbauxdim$, the periodic extension of $u$ is denoted by $\hat{u} : \R \rightarrow \R^\bbauxdim$, \emph{i.e.}, $\hat{u}(t) = u(\Mod(t, \bbperiod))$.
Let $\beta,\gamma\in\R_{>0}$, and $\bbstate_n$ be the solution of \eqref{eq:nlall_xn}. Fix a final time $t_f=a\bbperiod$ with $a\in\N$ large enough to include a ``reasonable'' number of periods, an arbitrary $v_0$ with values in $v_0(t)\in\R^\bbauxdim$, fix $(\lambda_n(t_f),\lambda_a(t_f))=(0,0)$, and define
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:nlall_deltadH}
  \delta(H_v(\bbstate_a(t),\lambda_a(t),v(t))) = \left( \int_{t_f-\bbperiod}^{t_f} H_v(\bbstate_a(t),\lambda_a(t),v(t)) H_v(\bbstate_a(t),\lambda_a(t),v(t))'\,dt \right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The proposed method is stated in \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep}, and its properties are characterized by the following theorem.

\begin{algorithm}[h!]
  \caption{Steady-state steepest descent algorithm \cite{masocco_dynamic_2024}.}
  \label{alg:nlall_sweep}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require $\beta,\,\gamma,\,\bbperiod,\,t_f,\,v_0,\,\bbstate_n$
    \Ensure $\tilde{v}$
    \State Let $t\in[0,t_f]$.
    \State $\tilde{v}^{(0)}(t) \gets v_0(t)$
    \For{$k=1,\dotsc$}
      \State $\bbstate_a^{(k)}(t) \gets$ solution of \eqref{eq:nlall_xa} with $\bbaux(t)=\tilde{v}^{(k-1)}(t)$
      \State $(\lambda_n^{(k)}(t),\lambda_a^{(k)}(t)) \gets$ backward integration of \eqref{eq:nlall_lambdadot} with
      \Statex \hspace{3.72cm}$(\bbstate_a(t),v(t))=(\bbstate_a^{(k)}(t),\tilde{v}^{(k-1)}(t))$
      \If{$\delta(H_v(\bbstate_a^{(k)}(t),\lambda_a^{(k)}(t),\tilde{v}^{(k-1)}(t))) < \beta$}
        \Return $\tilde{v}(t) \gets \tilde{v}^{(k-1)}(t)$
      \Else
        \State $u^{(k)}(t) \gets \tilde{v}^{(k-1)}(t) - \gamma\,H_v(\bbstate_a^{(k)}(t),\lambda_a^{(k)}(t),\tilde{v}^{(k-1)}(t))$
        \State $u_p^{(k)}(\tau) \gets u^{(k)}(t_f - \bbperiod + \tau)$, with $\tau\in[0,\bbperiod]$
        \State $\tilde{v}^{(k)}(t) \gets \hat{u}^{(k)}_{p}(t)$ \Comment{Periodic extension.}
      \EndIf
    \EndFor
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}
  \label{thm:nlall_main}
  Consider system \eqref{eq:bbsys}, fix $\alpha>0$ (large enough), and suppose that \Cref{ass:bboveract,ass:bbiss,ass:bbssresp} hold. Given $v_0 : \R \rightarrow \R^{\bbauxdim}$, and $t_f=a\bbperiod$, there exists a sequence $\{\gamma^{(k)}\}$, $\gamma^{(k)}\in\R_{>0}$ such that the sequence of $\{\Tilde{v}^{(k)}(t)\}$, $t\in\R_{\geq 0}$, produced by \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} converges to an extremal solution of \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont}. Furthermore, if \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont} admits an optimal solution $v^{\star}$ which is the unique extremal, then
  \begin{align}
      \label{eq:nlall_limnu}
      \lim_{k\rightarrow +\infty} \left\Vert \Tilde{v}^{(k)}(t) - v^{\star}(t)\right\Vert & = 0, & t\in\R_{\geq 0}.
  \end{align}
  \closestatement
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  The existence of the sequence $\{\gamma^{(k)}\}$, as well as its convergence properties, are guaranteed by the Steepest Descent Algorithm (see \cite[Sec. 6.2]{kirk_optimal_1970}), properly adapted as in \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} to face \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:nlall_algparams}
  In practical scenarios, the update step $\gamma$ may be selected as a constant value together with a suitably defined stopping criterion evaluating the norm of the gradient at each step, trading asymptotic convergence for implementation simplicity. Consequently, if the magnitude of the update step is large the algorithm may not converge. Furthermore, the feasibility of \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} may also depend on the value of the parameter $\alpha$. Recalling \eqref{eq:nlall_lambdaadot}, a large value made the backward integration numerically unfeasible in the simulations that have been performed.\closeremark
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  \label{rem:nlall_dt}
  Recall that, as noted in \cite{kirk_optimal_1970}, another parameter of interest concerning the numerical implementations of \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} is the sampling interval with respect to which the time interval $[0,t_f]$ is discretized. The magnitude of the sampling interval must usually be small to allow for a feasible approximation of the continuous-time dynamics involved in \Cref{prb:ssinv} and has direct repercussions on the computational complexity of the integration steps.\closeremark
\end{remark}

The choice of the update step in \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep} is a crucial design task. Many heuristic techniques have emerged in the literature to improve the convergence of this method, in particular thanks to its extensive diffusion in finite-dimensional optimization fields such as, \emph{e.g.}, machine learning for the training of neural networks \cite{goodfellow_deep_2016}.

The ADAM scheme yields the update rule given in \Cref{alg:nlall_adam}. With an abuse of notation, the variables $s$ and $r$ store at each iteration, respectively, $H_v$ and $H_v \odot H_v$ of the current and of the previous iterations. Note also that the operation $(\cdot)^{1/2}$ on $\hat{r}$, as well as the operation $(\cdot)^{-1}$ on $(\hat{r}^{1/2}+\delta)$, reported on line 16 of \Cref{alg:nlall_adam}, are both performed component-wise. The values $\rho_1$ for $s$, and $\rho_2$ for $r$, constitute a measure of the importance of the past history, which is then used for the proper evaluation of the update step. In particular, an appropriate choice of these parameters allows one to discard all the information related to previous iterations, thus improving the update step by relying only on the most recent information. It is worth mentioning that this scheme adjusts the update step dynamically during the iterations, differently from the scheme provided by \Cref{alg:nlall_sweep}.

\begin{algorithm}[h!]
	\caption{Steady-state ADAM algorithm \cite{masocco_dynamic_2024}.}
	\label{alg:nlall_adam}
	\begin{algorithmic}[1]
		\Require $\beta,\,\gamma,\,\bbperiod,\,t_f,\,v_0(t),\,\bbstate_n(t),\,\rho_1,\,\rho_2,\,\delta$
		\Ensure $\tilde{v}(t)$
		\State let $t\in[0,t_f]$
		\State $\tilde{v}^{(0)}(t) \gets v_0(t)$
		\State $s^{(0)}(t)\gets0$
		\State $r^{(0)}(t)\gets0$
		\For{$k=1,\dotsc$}
			\State $\bbstate_a^{(k)}(t) \gets$ solution of \eqref{eq:nlall_xa} with $\bbaux(t)=\tilde{v}^{(k-1)}(t)$
			\State $(\lambda_n^{(k)}(t),\lambda_a^{(k)}(t)) \gets$ backward integration of \eqref{eq:nlall_lambdadot} with
      \Statex \hspace{3.72cm}$(\bbstate_a(t),v(t))=(\bbstate_a^{(k)}(t),\tilde{v}^{(k-1)}(t))$
			\If{$\delta(H_v(\bbstate_a^{(k)}(t),\lambda_a^{(k)}(t),\tilde{v}^{(k-1)}(t))) < \beta$}
				\Return $\tilde{v}(t) \gets \tilde{v}^{(k-1)}(t)$
			\Else
				\State let $\tau \in [0, \bbperiod]$, $\Bar{\tau} = t_f - \bbperiod + \tau$
				\State $H_v(\Bar{\tau}) \gets H_v(\bbstate_a^{(k)}(\Bar{\tau}),\lambda_a^{(k)}(\Bar{\tau}),\tilde{v}^{(k-1)}(\Bar{\tau}))$
				\State $s^{(k)}(\Bar{\tau})\gets\rho_1s^{(k-1)}(\Bar{\tau})+(1-\rho_1)H_v(\Bar{\tau})$
				\State $r^{(k)}(\Bar{\tau})\gets\rho_2r^{(k-1)}(\Bar{\tau})+(1-\rho_2)H_v(\Bar{\tau}) \odot H_v(\Bar{\tau})$
				\State $\hat{s}(\Bar{\tau}) \gets s(\Bar{\tau})/(1-\rho_1^k)$
				\State $\hat{r}(\Bar{\tau}) \gets r(\Bar{\tau})/(1-\rho_2^k)$
				\State $\Delta u^{(k)}(\Bar{\tau})\gets-\gamma\,\hat{s}(\Bar{\tau})\odot((\hat{r}(\Bar{\tau}))^{1/2}+\delta)^{-1}$
				\State $u^{(k)}(\Bar{\tau}) \gets \tilde{v}^{(k-1)}(\Bar{\tau}) + \Delta u^{(k)}(\Bar{\tau}) $
				\State $u_p^{(k)}(\tau) \gets u^{(k)}(\Bar{\tau})$
				\State $\tilde{v}^{(k)}(t) \gets \hat{u}^{(k)}_{p}(t)$ \Comment{Periodic extension.}
			\EndIf
		\EndFor
	\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{example}
  Consider the following continuous-time exosystem of the form given in \eqref{eq:bbexosys}:
  \begin{subequations}
    \label{eq:nlall_simexo}
    \begin{align}
      \dot{\bbexostate}(t) &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0\\ -\omega^2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \bbexostate(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\\
      \bbref(t) &= \begin{bmatrix} \bbexostate_1(t) + \bbexostate_3(t) \\ \bbexostate_2(t) \end{bmatrix}, & t\in\R_{\geq 0},
    \end{align}
  \end{subequations}
  with $\omega=2\pi f$, $f=0.2$, and $\bbexostate_0 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \omega & 2 \end{bmatrix}'$. Hence, consider the following continuous-time system of the form \eqref{eq:bbsys}:
  \begin{subequations}
    \label{eq:nlall_simplant}
    \begin{align}
      \dot{\bbstate}(t) &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \bbstate_1(t)+\bbstate_2(t) \\ \bbstate_1(t)-\bbstate_2(t) \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} e^{\bbstate_2(t)} & 1 \\ e^{\bbstate_2(t)} & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} (h_c(\bbstate(t),r(t))+\bbaux(t)), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\\
      \bboutp(t) &= h_c(\bbstate(t),\bbref(t)) + \bbaux(t), & t\in\R_{\geq 0},\\
      \bbouto(t) &= (1+\bbstate_3(t))^3, & t\in\R_{\geq 0},
    \end{align}
  \end{subequations}
  where $h_c : \R^3\times\R^3 \rightarrow \R^2$ is a given feedback control law, and $\bbaux : \R \rightarrow \R^2$ is the auxiliary input, to be determined as an extremal solution to \Cref{prb:nlall_optcont}. The control objective in this example is that of tracking, with the regulated output $\bbouto(t)$, the reference input $\bbref_1(t)=\bbexostate_1(t)+\bbexostate_3(t)=\sin(\omega t)+2$. To highlight the optimization action operated by the auxiliary input $\bbaux$, in a similar way with respect to the previous linear case, the control law $h_c(\bbstate(t),\bbref(t))$ has been designed as a feedback-linearizing input operating only on the first input, setting the second input to zero since an inspection of \eqref{eq:nlall_simplant} reveals that the system has relative degree $r=3$ from the first component of the input, affecting the system via the input map $\bbinmap_1(\chi(t)) = \begin{bmatrix} e^{\bbstate_2(t)} & e^{\bbstate_2(t)} & 0 \end{bmatrix}'$, to the output $\bbouto$. For a description of the synthesis procedure see \cite[Chap. 4]{isidori_nonlinear_1995}.
